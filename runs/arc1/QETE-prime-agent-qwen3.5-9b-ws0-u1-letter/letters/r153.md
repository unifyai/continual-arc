## General principles that transfer to unseen tasks

**Colors in ARC are not semantic — structure is.** Almost every rule I solved reduces to one of a handful of structural templates. When you see a new task, check these in order:

- **Background detection**: the most common color is usually the background. Objects are connected components of non-background.
- **Enclosed regions**: flood-fill background from the border; background components that don't touch the border are "enclosed pockets." Common transformations: recolor them, fill them, count them. (task-3f4fb1a9: fill enclosed *rectangular* background pockets with 4, with a special extra case for 2-tall pockets.)
- **Symmetries and tilings**: mirror (reverse rows/columns), rotate, or transpose a block and stack it (task-794e60f6: TL=input, TR=rot90cw, BL=rot90ccw, BR=rot180; task-73499f5a: mirror-row + double-width, stacked as block + reversed + block).
- **Periodicity**: if colored cells lie on lines (rows, columns, diagonals/anti-diagonals), the answer is often a cyclic fill along the perpendicular coordinate with period = number of lines (task-2e29965a).
- **Marker/rare color**: the least-common color often "draws" or "stamps." Count its positions per row/column; extend, connect, or replicate it. (task-5c839a47: fill 3 strictly between min and max dot per row/column; task-b7641a67: fill background between two same-colored cells in a row with a third color; task-8625f9bc: fill background on the bounding box of the marker and gaps ≤2 between marker runs.)
- **Stamps/copies**: find a template object (usually the largest or rarest component), find anchor cells or valid placements, stamp the template (task-1b7868f1: stamp the largest non-bg component's bbox centered at each other non-bg cell; task-770f2daf: stamp the rare-color shape at every offset where all its cells are background).
- **Separators/blocks**: solid single-color rows/columns divide the grid into blocks. Rules often reduce to per-block voting, comparison of A vs B blocks, or extracting one block (task-026adbf3: split into two same-size blocks, output 3 where they agree else the common color; task-48898d06: output per-block presence of stray color; task-67dd046a: downsample 3×3 blocks by mode excluding color 5; task-bb30ebc2: output corner-marker colors where uniform).
- **Geometry shifts**: shift everything down one row (task-efc2fba8: shift non-bg down one, top row becomes background), or slide objects toward a line until adjacent, then draw a full line on the far side (task-77262100).
- **Two-color remaps**: with only two colors, often map higher→itself, lower→0, or invert (task-2264bd4e). Simple, don't overthink.
- **Line-detection**: rows/columns that are entirely one color get recolored wholesale (task-47ed202e).

**When a component is "marked" with color 4 or 2, that's usually the output color, not part of the logic.** The colors 1, 2, 3, 4 recur constantly as output-assignment colors across entirely different rules. Don't read meaning into *which* number; read it from the demos.

**Write candidates, test mentally on all demos, and prefer the simplest rule that reproduces all of them.** A rule that needs an ad-hoc exception for one demo pair is usually wrong. Conversely, a rule that leaves one demo unexplained is definitely wrong — that demo is telling you about the general mechanism, not an edge case. E.g., in task-3f4fb1a9 the "non-rectangular pockets left unchanged" detail and the "bottom wall also becomes 4 when pocket is 2-tall" detail were both load-bearing; only running against every demo revealed that.

**Component analysis toolkit.** Have ready (and reuse from my files above — `task-d4079843.py` and `task-b62da9e0.py` contain solid BFS/stack flood-fill helpers):

- 4-connected components per color; components of the union of non-background colors (8-connected sometimes — check demos).
- Bounding boxes, solidity (does area == bbox area?), perimeter shapes (hollow rectangles: `len(comp) == 2*(h+w-2)`).
- Shape canon for twin detection: `tuple(sorted((y-min_y, x-min_x)))` — exact-translation twins share this canon.
- Most-common color, least-common color, per-row/per-column position lists.

**Search strategy for unknown rules.** If a one-pass structural guess fails, try in this order:
1. Recolorings based on counts/positions (rarest color, mode, neighbors).
2. Stamping/tiling with some detected template.
3. Sorting/moving objects by size or position.
4. Drawing lines: connect pairs of dots, extend runs, fill between extremes. Half the tasks I saw were "something between/along the extent of the rarest color."
5. Grid subdivision by uniform lines, then per-cell aggregation.

## Warnings from my failures

- **task-5997d753** (8 wrong attempts, unsolved): a rule about partial-pattern groups of the pattern color; my reproducing-everything program still predicted the test wrong. Lesson: when demos show a pattern-repeat structure, the *alignment/offset* between repeats is the crux; enumerate offsets and pick the one minimizing conflicts, and be suspicious if the test has a different number of groups than demos.
- **task-5c839a47**: identical trap, different flavor — test structure (one dot) outside the demo distribution. Request demos *immediately* when the test grid's object inventory differs from demos'.

Note in the failed file *which hypotheses remain untried* — that note in my `task-1b7868f1_failed.json` is exactly the kind of breadcrumb that saves a turn.

## Final advice

The single highest-value habit: **before every submission, print your program's output for the test input and look at it as a human.** Does the change it makes look "meaningful" — symmetric, aligned, complete? Most wrong answers I produced were technically executing a plausible rule but producing visibly lopsided or half-finished output. ARC answers look *designed*. If your prediction looks accidental, it's wrong.

Second habit: **name your rule in one sentence in a memory file after each task, solved or not.** Over a long stream, your own notes become a rule library. The templates above came from exactly those notes.

You have far less capacity than I do, so lean on the library, on careful code, and on verification-by-execution rather than cleverness. Methodical beats brilliant in this stream.

Good luck — the tasks are fair, and the demos always contain the whole rule if you look hard enough.
