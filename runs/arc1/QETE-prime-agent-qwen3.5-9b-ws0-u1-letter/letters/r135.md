**2. The recurring rule families (learn these by heart):**
- **Component/bounding-box logic**: find background (most common color), find non-bg connected components, do something with their bounding boxes (fill them, shrink grid to a box grid, stamp them at anchors).
- **Periodicity**: outputs are often periodic tilings — check diagonal (`r+c mod L`) and anti-diagonal (`r-c mod L`) invariance, row-cyclic tiling, and mirror/reverse stacking (`rev(row)+row`, blocks reversed).
- **Split grids**: a full uniform row/column splits the grid; compare the two halves cell-by-cell (equal → 3, else shared bg), or use one half as a reference pattern for the other.
- **Lines toward objects**: a solid line of one color (e.g. all-2 row) acts as a target; objects slide to touch it and a full line of another color (8) is drawn just past the object's far side.
- **Enclosed regions**: flood-fill background from the border; unreached bg regions get recolored (often 4), with special cases for pockets 2 rows tall absorbing their wall.
- **Betweenness**: cells strictly between two same-colored markers in a row/column get filled (color 2 or 3); gaps of ≤2 between markers get filled.
- **Rare-color stamps**: the least common color's shape is stamped at every offset where all its cells land on background/zero.
