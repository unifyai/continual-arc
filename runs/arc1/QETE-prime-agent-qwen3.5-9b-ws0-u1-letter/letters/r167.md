## Requesting demos

Demos are nearly free. My costliest failures (task-5c839a47) came from having a rule that fit 10 demo pairs perfectly but failed on a *degenerate* test input (a single dot). Lesson: when your rule has a special case you've never seen — one instance of the pattern, zero instances, ties in a vote — **request more demos rather than submitting a guess about the edge case**. Every wrong attempt costs more than a demo.

## Rule families I actually saw (with the tell for each)

Most tasks are one of these. Match the tell, then confirm on demos.

1. **Symmetry/reflection construction** (task-73499f5a, task-794e60f6). Output dims are a simple multiple of input dims (2×2, 3×1, 2×2 blocks). Tell: output H and W are exact multiples, and sub-blocks look like copies. Try: block + mirrored block (horizontally, vertically), quadrants = input / rot90 / rot90ccw / rot180, or reversed-row-order stacking.

2. **Line/cycle coloring** (task-2e29965a, task-47ed202e, task-8625f9bc). Sparse colored cells lie on straight lines (rows, columns, diagonals, antidiagonals). Tell: cells of one color are collinear. The output often cycles the line colors along the perpendicular key modulo the number of lines. Check `i+j`, `i-j`, `i`, `j` as the line key. Related: full uniform rows/columns get repainted a constant color.

3. **Grid-line partition / block summary** (task-bb30ebc2, task-48898d06, task-67dd046a, task-026adbf3). Full solid rows/columns of one color divide the grid into blocks. Tell: one color appears in complete lines. Output is usually a small grid, one cell per block, computed from block contents: majority non-separator color, parity of a stray color, whether two half-blocks agree. For agreement tasks: output "3" where the two halves match, else the blocks' shared background.

4. **Component stamping / template propagation** (task-1b7868f1, task-8d84fe63, task-770f2daf). One object is the "template" (usually the largest component, or the one with rarest color, or the one whose neighborhood is a clean anchor); it gets stamped at every other anchor cell, or into every empty box of matching size. Tell: one repeated pattern plus several trigger dots. My working rule: stamp the template's *bounding box* translated so its center maps onto the anchor, overwriting everything (including 0s and holes) — that reproduced a stubborn task after I stopped trying to be clever about which cells to copy.

5. **Enclosed pockets** (task-3f4fb1a9, task-aa5a69a0, task-b62da9e0). Flood-fill background from the border; interior background regions get filled (with a constant, or a parity-dependent color, or only if rectangular). Tell: hollow rectangles / letter-like shapes on background. Note: "enclosed" = no border contact. One variant only filled rectangular pockets and left irregular ones alone.

6. **Object moves toward a line** (task-77262100). A full line of a rare color (often 2); the object slides until adjacent to it, and a full line of another color (often 8) is drawn just outside the object's far side. Handle both orientations and both sides of the line.

7. **Gap filling between markers** (task-5c839a47, task-b7641a67, task-8625f9bc). Between the min and max positions of a marker color (per row or per column, or between consecutive markers along a line), fill background cells with a constant (3 or 2). Careful with "strictly between" and with lone markers.

8. **Diagonal ray extension** (task-3b5f776f). Runs of length ≥2 of the same color extend along their diagonal/antidiagonal through background until blocked. Cheap to implement, easy to miss as a hypothesis.

9. **Relative-distance recoloring** (task-0f6e5a2f). A singleton "special" object (an L, an arrow, a thin angle) plus exact-translation twins of some shape; the twin farther from (or nearer to) the special object gets recolored to its color. Compute min Manhattan distance between component cell sets.

10. **Shift everything down one** (task-efc2fba8). Trivially simple tasks exist. If output looks like input translated by one, just do it — don't overthink.

11. **Copy correction via comparison** (task-026adbf3). Two near-identical regions; the output marks their differences or their agreements.

## Concrete algorithmic advice

- **Background = most common color.** Almost always. Second color = marker/line. Rare colors = triggers/seeds.
- **4-connectivity for objects; 8-connectivity only if 4- doesn't split things sensibly.** Try both if components look wrong.
- **Canonicalize shapes** by `tuple(sorted((y-min y, x-min x)))` and group by it. Many rules are about "same shape, different position."
- **Bounding boxes are your friend**: bbox center for stamping, bbox perimeter for filling, bbox size for matching boxes.
- When a rule depends on "which of a pair," distance from a special singleton with min over all cell pairs is usually the measure.
- Write transform programs that are pure functions of the grid and raise `ValueError` when the assumed structure (separator, twin pair, etc.) isn't found — then your fallback logic is visible and you know to try a different family rather than silently producing garbage.

## Judging a test input you can't verify

Before submitting on the test, ask: does my rule have an undefined case for *this* input? Specifically: what happens with zero, one, or an odd number of the trigger objects? Are there ties (e.g., two candidate pairs equally distant)? If yes, request a demo if you can, else pick the most *conservative* behavior (usually: leave things unchanged). Note "identity" is rarely right when any structure exists, but it's a decent tiebreak when the trigger is absent.

If a submission is rejected, don't thrash between variations of the same idea. Count which structural choice was probably wrong (which cell got recolored? which direction?) and change exactly one thing. Track tried variants in the memory file — I kept a "failed variants" note and it saved me from resubmitting duplicates (sub1 == sub3 happened to me once; that's a pure wasted attempt).

## Mindset

The rules in this stream are almost always simple once seen — a translation, a fill, a recolor, a stamp. The difficulty is *generating the right hypothesis*, not implementing it. So spend your effort on enumeration: dimensions of the output, multiples and mirror structure, complete lines, enclosed regions, rare colors. Two minutes of looking at the demo output's shape and palette usually narrows it to one family.
