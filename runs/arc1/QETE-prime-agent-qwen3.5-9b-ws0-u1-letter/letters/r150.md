## Rules I've actually seen (pattern library)

Most tasks reduce to a handful of shapes. When a new task appears, check these first:

- **Tiling / fractal stamps**: seed shape (often the least-common color or largest component) stamped at every offset where it fits in background (task-770f2daf), or a bbox stamped centered on every non-bg cell (task-1b7868f1), or quadrant reflections producing a 2n×2n grid (task-794e60f6), or mirror+double each row and stack reversed/original/reversed (task-73499f5a).
- **Separator lines**: full uniform rows/columns split the grid into blocks. Then: compare two blocks cellwise (equal → 3, else shared color — task-026adbf3); count stray colored cells per block (≥2 → 1 else bg — task-48898d06); read a corner-intersection mini-grid (task-bb30ebc2); compare the two halves' dominant colors (task-707635df); an object slides until it touches a 2-line and an 8-line is drawn on its far side (task-77262100).
- **Enclosed regions / pockets**: flood-fill background components; those not touching the border are pockets — recolor (task-3f4fb1a9, which also had a subtle "2-tall pocket extends one row down" clause; non-rectangular pockets were left alone).
- **Diagonal/run extension**: same-color runs of length ≥2 extend along r−c and r+c until blocked (task-3b5f776f).
- **Line-of-sight fills**: cells strictly between the min and max of the rare color per row/column become a fixed color (task-5c839a47); background cells between two same-colored cells in a row become a different color (task-b7641a67); gaps ≤2 between markers along rows/cols plus the marker bbox perimeter get filled (task-8625f9bc).
- **Cleanup**: rectangles of size ≥3 are kept and everything else becomes background (task-b62da9e0); uniform rows/cols become all-2 (task-47ed202e); color mapping by rank (task-2264bd4e).
- **Periodic patterns**: colors constant along anti-diagonals/rows/diagonals determine a full-grid periodic fill (task-2e29965a — try each of the three line families and check consistency).
- **Twin selection**: among exactly-two-occurrence twin shapes, recolor the one farther from a distinguished "arrow" object (task-0f6e5a2f).
- **Row shift**: all non-bg cells shift down one (task-efc2fba8). Beware: my first version was "shift rows down, top becomes bg[0][0]" — nearly the same, but the boundary details mattered. Get edge behavior exactly right.

## Hard-won lessons

- **Degenerate inputs kill rules.** Task-5c839a47: my rule worked on 10/10 demos (multiple dots per line) but the test had a *single* dot, and I burned 8 attempts guessing. If a rule's precondition ("≥2 of X") might not hold on the test, decide the degenerate behavior *before* submitting, and if demos never exercise it, request demos or pick the most conservative option (often identity or background-only). Any task whose demos all show rich structure may be testing the sparse case.
- **Don't submit an unverified guess.** The only submission that should ever leave your hands is one your program produces and that reproduces all kept demos.
- **Exactness matters everywhere**: 4- vs 8-connectivity, whether the separating line itself is included, whether a run's endpoints count as "between," inclusive vs exclusive bounds, what happens at grid edges. Off-by-one and edge-handling are the most common cause of "reproduces demos but fails test." When two readings both fit demos, prefer the one whose edge behavior is simplest and symmetric.
- **Identify roles robustly**: background = most common color; the "special" color is usually least common or the unique one with a different component count. But guard for ties and missing roles (a program that crashes on an unexpected grid scores zero).
- **Write general programs, not lookup tables.** The grader runs your `transform` on a *new* input. Symmetry-check your rule: would it still work if the object were on the other side, rotated, or a different color? Tasks often test the mirror case of what demos show (my d4079843 program handles both orientations and both sides — that's why it passed).
- **Keep failed attempts in a `_failed.json` with a note** (see my task-1b7868f1 file): what was tried, what's untried. This prevents resubmitting a near-duplicate.

## Practical habits

- Parse grids as lists of lists of ints; components via flood fill (decide connectivity per task — most of mine were 4-connected for objects, but check).
- Always work on a copy: `out = [row[:] for row in grid]`.
- When several hypotheses fit, rank them by simplicity (fewer free parameters, no magic constants) and by how naturally they handle edge cases.
- When the rule is genuinely ambiguous and attempts remain, a demo request is better than a coin-flip submission.
