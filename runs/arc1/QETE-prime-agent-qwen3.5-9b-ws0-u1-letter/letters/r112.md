## Heuristics that recur constantly

- **Background = most common color.** Almost always; occasionally task-0f6e5a2f style tasks need "minority non-zero" instead.
- **Rank colors by frequency.** Rare colors usually mark "objects," "dots," or "clues"; the second-most-common is often a frame, line, or separator color.
- **Separator rows/columns** (a full row/column of one uniform color) are a huge structural cue: split the grid into blocks, compare blocks, tile blocks, or AND the two sides. Several tasks hinge entirely on this.
- **Connected components** (4-connectivity usually; 8-connectivity for task-1b7868f1 and task-3b5f776f) are the standard way to find objects. Check both when bounding boxes look off.
- **Bounding boxes** of components are the second-biggest recurring tool: fill, stamp, compare, or mirror about them.
- **Counting parity** (even→2, odd→7) shows up. Don't dismiss arbitrary-looking output colors; look at counts and sizes.
- **"Between two same things in a row/column"** is a recurring fill condition (b7641a67, 5c839a47, 8625f9bc gaps).
- Symmetry/tiling tasks (794e60f6, 73499f5a, 8d84fe63): when the output is larger than the input by an exact integer factor in both dimensions, suspect rotations, reflections, or tiling of the input.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Don't submit on the first demo alone** if there's any ambiguity in the rule. One wrong guess costs more than a demo request.
- **Don't overfit the general shape of a rule.** task-3f4fb1a9's rule has a subtle exception (2-row pockets absorbing a wall). Verify edge cases: pockets touching the border, single-cell shapes, ties in size.
- **Watch for ties.** "Tiebreak: larger size" or "nearest to the arrow" rules needed explicit tiebreakers to reproduce demos.
- **Components with holes**: a "rectangle" check should be `len(comp) == area of bbox` to catch hollow vs solid — that's how aa5a69a0 works.
- When the same rule could be applied "majority vs minority" or "min vs max color", **test both interpretations against every demo**. Role assumptions are the most common silent failure.
- Failed tasks stay on record: `task-5997d753` and `task-1b7868f1_failed.json` show the shapes of rules that were *wrong*. Read the failed grids — the correct rule is often a small variation (e.g., using a different anchor or a clean anchor cell) rather than a new idea.

## Practical tips

- Use small helper functions: `components(grid, color, conn)`, `bbox(comp)`, `Counter` for background, band/segment splitting for separators. I've rewritten these a dozen times; a tidy library speeds everything.
- Verify with a quick script: `for pair in json.load(f): assert transform(pair['input']) == pair['output']`.
- When writing memory entries, state the rule in one sentence plus the exceptions. Your future self reads them at speed.
